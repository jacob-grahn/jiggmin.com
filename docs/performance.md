# Visibility performance

Run `npm run perf:visibility` from the repository root. This loads the production room GLB (725,154 triangles), its exported camera, and cartridge-sized probes. It measures batches of five tabletop probes and 24 scattered probes, verifies accelerated visibility against ordinary Three.js raycasting, repeats the comparison after moving the props, and measures the physics step separately. Timings are CPU-only in Node on the local machine; they are not browser frame rates or GPU timings.

The original recovery loop queried the detailed room for every loose cartridge every 200 ms. Each ray could scan the room's entire triangle list, with up to six rays per cartridge. A local September 26 run measured median batches of 129 ms for five tabletop cartridges and 2,371 ms for 24 scattered cartridges. With the spatial index those batches took 0.09 ms and 0.89 ms, respectively (maximums 2.2 ms and 3.53 ms). Timings vary with machine load. Index construction took 480 ms once during scene initialization.

Room and reactive prop meshes now use local-space triangle BVHs. Rigid prop motion keeps the index valid. Visibility and pointer picking request the nearest hit only. Cartridges in motion, being dragged, or animating do not need visibility checks for return-to-shelf recovery. Recovery remains disabled during game play. Visibility checks now run for at most one eligible cartridge per second, in round-robin order. Only cartridges grabbed or inserted during this visit are eligible, and any subsequent movement restarts a five-second quiet period. Once eligible, a hidden cartridge returns on its next check; there is no additional five-second timer or catch-up burst.

The browser bundle and license in `web/vendor/three-mesh-bvh/` are copied unmodified from the pinned npm dependency's `build/index.module.js` and `LICENSE`. Keep these in sync when upgrading. The implementation follows the library's [accelerated raycast API](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh/blob/master/API.md).
